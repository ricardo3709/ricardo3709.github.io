Xinyu Wang (Ricardo)

Ph.D. researcher in Transportation Engineering at the University of Sydney

I study how AI and operations research can improve urban mobility. Most days, that means helping shared rides think a few steps ahead.

My work combines reinforcement learning, network optimization, and city-scale simulation to answer questions such as how shared rides should be matched and routed, where passengers should be picked up and dropped off, and how to balance efficiency with fairness.

I’m part of TransportLab in the School of Civil Engineering, supervised by Dr. Andres Fielbaum and Prof. David Levinson. My research draws on real-world demand and networks from Manhattan, Utrecht, and Canberra.

TR Part A2027New

Who should pick the meeting point? Efficiency–fairness trade-offs between operator and users in ridepooling

Xinyu Wang*, Andres Fielbaum

Transportation Research Part A: Policy and Practice, 215, 105326. Published online October 23, 2026.

When the platform and its passengers would pick different meeting points, who should decide? Across Manhattan, Utrecht, and Canberra, operator choice serves more requests, while passenger choice cuts walking per pick-up or drop-off leg roughly threefold. Disagreement is spatially concentrated, more frequent at drop-off, and weighs most on riders least willing to walk.

TR Part C2026

RL-based anticipatory routing and matching in on-demand ridepooling

Xinyu Wang*, Andres Fielbaum

Transportation Research Part C: Emerging Technologies, 192, 105898.

A graph neural network actor–critic framework learns how strongly to anticipate future demand when routing and matching shared rides, with cross-city transfer from Manhattan to Utrecht.

TR Part C2026

Where should the last passenger be dropped off? Anticipatory walking in ridepooling

Xinyu Wang*, Andres Fielbaum

Transportation Research Part C: Emerging Technologies, 187, 105645.

Choosing the last passenger’s drop-off with future trips in mind improves ridepooling service. The method combines sampled future demand with network structure and is evaluated in three cities.
